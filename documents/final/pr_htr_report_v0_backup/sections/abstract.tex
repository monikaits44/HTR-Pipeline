\begin{abstract}
Handwritten Text Recognition (HTR) on the IAM benchmark is close to saturated on character error rate (CER): a compact CNN--RNN--CTC baseline reaches $4.5$--$4.9\%$~CER on the Aachen split. What is not saturated is \emph{attention interpretability}. Self-attention maps from Vision Transformer (ViT) recognizers are noisy and dominated by a small number of high-norm ``sink'' patch tokens, which limits their usefulness for downstream tasks such as writer identification. This report tests whether the register-token mechanism of Darcet~\etal, originally proposed for classification ViTs, transfers to a CTC-based HTR system and improves attention quality without hurting recognition. We build a CNN-stem Vision Transformer with a dual BiLSTM+CNN CTC head (ViT-RGTS~v2, $9.5$M parameters), sweep the register count over $R \in \{0, 2, 4, 8, 16\}$, and compare against the CNN--RNN baseline, a pretrained TorchVision ViT-B/16 fine-tuned with a two-group learning rate, and TrOCR fine-tuned with layer-wise learning-rate decay. In total we run $63$ training experiments (runs~143--205, up to $150$ epochs each) on IAM and READ2016, with three seeds for a variance floor and $16$ synthetic-pretraining runs on ${\sim}950$K rendered lines.

Two findings organize the report. First, on the recognition axis the register-token count is close to neutral: the test CER varies by only $0.24$~pp across $R = 0$ to $R = 16$ on IAM (range $5.74$--$5.98\%$, tightening to $5.60$--$5.70\%$ once the delivered SWA-averaged model is evaluated) and by $0.09$~pp on READ2016, both within a seed-noise floor of $\pm 0.08$~pp measured over three seeds. This matches Darcet~\etal's own observation for classification ViTs that registers are an interpretability intervention rather than an accuracy intervention. Second, on the attention axis the claim only partially transfers: register tokens visibly absorb the artifact-attention pattern seen in the $R{=}0$ ViT, and per-character attention becomes sharper and better aligned with the CTC decoding column, but a residual sink pattern intrinsic to CTC self-attention remains and requires an additional CTC-column prior to suppress. The best overall recognizer stays the CNN--RNN baseline with SWA ($4.50\%$~CER, $15.30\%$~WER, $7.36$M parameters), and the best interpretable recognizer is ViT-RGTS~v2 with four registers and SWA ($5.70\%$~CER, $18.70\%$~WER, $9.48$M parameters). Two ablations collapse to ${\sim}73\%$~CER when the CNN stem is removed or when a legacy row-major patch layout is used, both isolating the CTC-monotonic-alignment constraint as the single load-bearing design decision for a Transformer HTR encoder. All models, configurations, and 63 experiment logs are released with the report.
\end{abstract}
